# Changelog

All notable changes to OxiGuitar. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The player's timeline (M4 slice 1, `oxiguitar-play`): a score as it plays, with repeats, alternate endings, D.C., D.S., codas and Fine in Guitar Pro's order, tempo changes (gradual ones exactly), swing and triplet feel, every note's start and end in seconds with its velocity and every effect (bends, the whammy bar, slides, hammer-ons, ties, let ring, grace notes, strums, tremolo picking, trills, harmonics, dead, ghost and palm-muted notes, vibrato, fades), the metronome and a bar of count-in. `oxiguitar-cli events FILE` prints it. Nothing plays it yet: the sound comes in the next slices.
- Plugin interface 1.3, its first part (M4 slice 2): a plugin can have a **settings window**, opened from Tools → Plugins with its Settings… button; add its own **section to a track's Properties…** (for the tracks it cares about), whose settings OxiGuitar saves beside the score in `FILE.oxg` (`song.gp3.oxg`), since no score format holds them, and undoes with the rest of the dialog; end a **footer message with a link** that opens in your browser (https only); and get **folders**: its own data and cache folders, and the `soundfonts` and `nam` folders beside `plugins`, which OxiGuitar creates when a plugin asks for them. `oxiguitar-cli convert` carries the `.oxg` file to the new file. The SDK re-exports serde_json.
- The player's engine (M4 slice 2, `oxiguitar-play`): it plays the timeline on its frames, at 25 to 200 % speed without changing pitch, with loops, seeking, the count-in and the metronome, mixes the tracks (volume, pan, mute, solo) and keeps the clock the cursor will follow. Plugin interface 1.3 gains instruments and effects on the audio thread, and the contract suite checks each one: notes start on time, and nothing allocates (or, on Linux, makes a system call) while it renders.
- OxiGuitar can make sound: audio-device plugins for PipeWire and ALSA (Linux), WASAPI (Windows) and Core Audio (macOS), in the basic plugins, and `oxiguitar-cli play FILE`, which plays a score from the terminal (from a bar, looping bars, at 25 to 200 % speed, with the count-in and the metronome) through the installed instruments. The instrument that plays real sounds, the SoundFont plugin, comes next; until then nothing installed plays a score.
- The SoundFont plugin (M4 slice 3, in the basic plugins): it plays every track with a SoundFont 2 sound set through rustysynth, the one in your `soundfonts` folder (beside `plugins`) or else the largest your system has, each string of a guitar on a channel of its own, every note within 0.15 ms of its time. On Debian, Ubuntu and Fedora the `oxiguitar-plugins-basic` package now recommends a General MIDI sound set (`fluid-soundfont-gm`). It plays SoundFont 3 files too (decoded once and kept in its cache folder) and DLS files, so Windows' and macOS's own sets play out of the box. Its settings window (Tools → Plugins…, SoundFont, Settings…) picks a set for guitars, basses, drums and everything else. The first time it plays with only your system's sets, the footer says so, once, with a link to the manual's new Better sounds page.
- The SoundFont plugin plays the effects: bends, the whammy bar and slides as pitch bend on their string, vibrato, hammer-ons and pull-offs carrying on the ringing string, palm mutes and dead notes damped, harmonics (with General MIDI's guitar harmonics on clean guitars), slap and pop on basses, swells and fades.
- `oxiguitar-cli play FILE --wav OUT` plays a score into a WAV file instead of a sound card.
- **The player in the window** (M4 slice 4): the Player tab plays the open score from the caret (Space, as in Guitar Pro 8), plays the selection and stops at its end, pauses (the caret stays on the beat heard), stops (the caret goes back where playing began), rewinds, loops the selection or the whole song, counts in a bar, clicks a metronome, plays at 25 to 200 % without changing pitch, and has a master level and the choice of output (kept for next time). Ctrl + Left and Right move a bar. The mixer in each Tracks row (volume, pan, mute, solo) changes the sound as it plays, and the track overview's playhead follows it. Sounds are made ready when a file opens, so Play doesn't wait. While the score plays it can't be edited: pause it to edit (Mateus, 25 Oct 2026). With no instrument, no sound set or no audio output, the controls are greyed out and the footer says what to get.
- The engine limits its output to −1 dBFS, without adding latency, so loud songs no longer clip; the usual master level is about +6 dB, in the window and in `oxiguitar-cli play`.
- The SDK's `Piece::pitch`: the whole array of pitch points, so an instrument can follow a note's curve from one call to the next.
- Help → About asks for donations: a **Donate with PayPal** button, which opens the page in your browser, and a QR code (Mateus, 25 Oct 2026). OxiGuitar still connects nowhere itself: the browser does, and only when you click.

### Changed

- Releases attach one zip of our plugins for each system (and the `oxiguitar-plugins-basic` .deb and .rpm) instead of every plugin as a file of its own, and the release notes open with which two files each system needs (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026).
- OxiGuitar downloads nothing, not even at your request: better sound sets (from the player, M4), models and plugins are files you put in a folder, and the manual says what to get and where (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026). The plans for a download button for SoundFonts are dropped.
- Building OxiGuitar needs Rust 1.99, from rustup rather than a distribution's package (Mateus, 24 Oct 2026). Plugins built with the SDK of v0.5.0 still need only 1.97.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-10-21

Milestone M3 (the editor) is complete: OxiGuitar edits everything TuxGuitar does, and more, and saves as .gp and .gp5. Plugin interface 1.2 is frozen with this release.

### Added

- Writing Guitar Pro 5 files (.gp5; M3 slice 7): the Guitar Pro 5 plugin now writes as well as reads (plugin interface 1.2), and Save and Save As… write .gp5, so a .gp5 file is saved as itself. Version 5.10, which Guitar Pro 5 and every later version, and TuxGuitar, open. What it can't hold is named with the first bar and track, as for .gp (FMT-3): extra staves and more than two voices, tuplets beyond the common ones, double dots, free time, section letters, tempo names and gradual tempos, left-hand tapping, text beyond 255 characters or Windows-1252, and more. Drums, tracks without strings and the second round trip are exact. Over the corpus, all 104 files are written and read back equal or with their loss named: 96 exactly and 8 with named losses, and every Guitar Pro 5 file of it comes back exactly (`cargo test -p oxiguitar-formats --test gp5_round_trip`). The editing gates run for .gp5 too, and `fixtures/guitar-pro-check/` holds every reference song as .gp5 as well as .gp, for checking in Guitar Pro.
- Effects (M3 slice 6): every effect of Guitar Pro's tablature (TAB-1) from its key and the palette's Effects group, on the note on the caret's string or the caret's beat, or on every note or beat selected; pressed again, it comes off. Guitar Pro 8's keys (X dead note, O ghost, ; and : accents, ! staccato, H hammer-on, S legato slide, V vibrato, P and Shift + P palm mute, I let ring, Y and Ctrl + Alt + Y harmonics, N trill, " tremolo picking, ) and $ tapping and slap, ( left-hand tapping, < fade in, G and Ctrl + Alt + G grace notes, Ctrl + D and Ctrl + U brushes, Shift + D and Shift + U pick strokes, Ctrl + Alt + W wide vibrato), and ours: T tapping, Alt + S shift slide, Shift + S slide out downwards, Alt + P pinch harmonic, Alt + T tapped harmonic (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026). Effects with a value start at a default: a trill with the next fret up in sixteenths, tremolo picking in thirty-seconds, a brush over a sixteenth, harmonics 12 up; grace notes are thirty-seconds. The palette's dynamics set the beat's dynamic. The tools light up for the caret's note and beat, and the palette's tools beyond TAB-1 say they come in M5. Every effect is held to the editing gates: random runs undo to the original, and saved as .gp read back equal.
- The bend editor (B, or the palette's Bend) and the whammy bar's (Ctrl + Alt + V): a type (Bend, Bend and release, Release, Prebend…; Dive, Dip, Return…), a pitch from ¼ tone to 3 tones, and the curve's points dragged on a grid of twelfths of the note by quarter tones, in the shape .gp files hold.
- The palette's Raise and Lower by a Semitone, Multiple Ties and Section tools work.
- Chord diagrams (M3 slice 6): the chord dialog (A, the palette's Chord and Chords, Edit → Chord…) with Guitar Pro 8's builder (root, type, extension, alterations, bass), the name it makes, and the ways to play the chord on the track's strings, easiest first; a grid to place notes, which names the chord they make; the track's chords to pick again. On a rest, a chord puts its notes in. The Inspector shows the beat's chord.
- Text above a beat (Shift + T, the palette's Text, Edit → Text…).
- The Song tab, beside the Inspector (M3 slice 6): the score's title, artist, subtitle, album, words, music, copyright, transcriber, notices and instructions, in Guitar Pro 8's order, each set as one step to undo.
- The palette's search field (Ctrl + F): only the tools whose names hold the words typed.
- Lyrics typed on the page, as in MuseScore (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026): Ctrl + T on a note opens a field under it; Space and hyphen go to the next note, Backspace in an empty field to the one before, Up and Down to another verse (five, as Guitar Pro), Enter or Escape end. Each syllable is one step to undo, and the lyrics stay as Guitar Pro writes them.
- The Inspector shows and changes the note on the caret's string and its beat (M3 slice 6): its effects as tickboxes, and the values the keys start at a default: accent, slides in and out, harmonic and its interval, trill speed and fret, left- and right-hand fingering, dynamic, tremolo speed, grace note, brush and its speed, pick stroke, tapping, slapping or popping; Edit… opens the bend and whammy-bar editor. Each change is one step to undo, and every one is in the editing gates.
- Tracks (M3 slice 5): a right-click on a track's name gives its menu, with Properties…, Transpose…, Add Track, Duplicate, Move Up, Move Down and Delete… (after a question). The track dialog (also Edit → Track Properties…) sets the name and short name, instrument, strings (3 to 10), tuning as a key and a pattern (Standard, Drop, Open D style, Open G style) or any notes, capo and frets, as one change; changing the strings keeps the notes' pitches or their frets, and a question says how many notes no string can play before leaving them out. Tunings are named compactly at the top of the page ("Tuning: Drop D"). Shown tracks stay shown when tracks are added, deleted or moved.
- Transposition (M3 slice 5), by semitones or by steps of the key, keeping the fingering or finding new frets near the old ones: the selection or the caret's beat (right-click on the page, or Edit → Transpose Selection…), a track (its menu), or every track with its key signatures (Tools → Transpose…). Drum tracks are never transposed.
- A right-click on the page gives the selection's commands: Transpose…, Bar…, Track Properties…, Cut, Copy and Paste. Outside the selection it first moves the caret there.
- Only the caret's voice is drawn in full ink; the other voices are greyed, on screen only (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026).
- The Inspector shows the caret's bar: for each voice, how many beats it holds against the time signature, in the signature's own unit ("3½ of 4"), whether it is full, short or too long and by how much, and how many beats were entered; and on which beat the caret's beat starts (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026). The Inspector now starts open.
- A double-click on a time signature opens the Bar dialog for its bar.
- Copy, cut and paste (M3 slice 4), OxiGuitar's own: beats copy within a voice and bars across tracks, Ctrl + V inserts at the caret, and Special Paste (Ctrl + Shift + V) pastes several times or replaces. Between drum and pitched tracks only the rhythm carries over. Edit → Go to Section moves the caret to a section.
- Bars and tuplets (M3 slice 4): Insert inserts a bar and Ctrl + Delete deletes bars; `[` and `]` open and close repeats, `|` puts a bar in free time, `/` makes triplets, and Custom N-tuplet takes any grouping. The Bar dialog (Ctrl + T, Ctrl + K, D, or the palette's bar tools) sets a bar's time and key signature, clef, tempo, feel, repeats with a count, alternate endings, section, directions, double bar line and free time as one change; signatures, clefs and feel hold until the next change.
- More note entry (M3 slice 3): on the standard staff a click gives the caret a line or space, Up and Down move it, and Enter puts a note there in the bar's key (on a guitar, on the string where its fret is lowest, nearest the caret's). File → New (Ctrl + N) starts a score of one bar with a steel-string guitar, and Right at the end of a score adds a bar. Alt + 1 to 4 move the beat to another voice, Ctrl + Shift + Delete clears beats, and the Edit menu gains Insert Beat, Delete Beats, Clear Beats, Clear Bar, Move Beat Left and Right, Move Beat to Voice and Remove Unused Voices.
- Entering notes (M3 slice 3), with Guitar Pro 8's keys: 0 to 9 put a fret on the caret's string (two digits in a row make one fret), R a rest, − and + a longer or shorter note, Shift + . and Ctrl + . one or two dots, L and Shift + L ties, Backspace deletes a note, Shift + Delete a beat, Ctrl + Insert inserts one, Alt + Shift + Up and Down move a note a semitone, Alt + Up and Down to the next string at the same pitch. After a short voice's last beat the caret has one more place, where typing adds a beat; typing in a voice the bar doesn't have starts it. The palette's note values, Rest, dots and Tie work and show the caret's beat. Every edit is one undoable change; tied notes follow the note they continue. Gates: random runs of edits over every fixture undo to the original and redo to the end, the edited score saved as .gp reads back equal, and a typed note shows on screen within 100 ms on the 300-bar band song (27 ms here). The manual's Editing page has a new section, Entering notes.
- Writing Guitar Pro 7 and 8 files (.gp), the editor's save format (M3 slice 2): the Guitar Pro 7–8 plugin now writes as well as reads, in the shape Guitar Pro 7.6 saves, claiming no particular version. Every track gets a sound from Guitar Pro's own library, drum tracks Guitar Pro's drum kit, and every chord diagram its chord spelled out from its name, which Guitar Pro needs. What a .gp file can't hold is named, with the first bar and track where it happens (FMT-3). A .gp file read and written comes back exactly; in the corpus, 50 of 104 files of every Guitar Pro version come back exactly and the rest name their losses, chiefly different dynamics on the notes of one beat (Guitar Pro 7 has one per beat).
- File → Save (Ctrl + S) and Save As… (Ctrl + Shift + S), and Save on the toolbar. Save keeps a file's own format unless that format can't hold everything in the score, or no installed plugin writes it; then the score is saved beside it as .gp, the fullest format OxiGuitar writes, after asking (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026). Before writing a file that loses part of the score, a question lists what, with the first bar and track (FMT-3), and, when the file the score came from is written over, what OxiGuitar couldn't keep when it opened it (FMT-2). Opening another file, quitting or closing the window with unsaved changes asks whether to save them. The window's title shows the file's name, marked while there are unsaved changes. The manual has a new page, Saving files.
- Plugin interface 1.2 (M3 slice 2), frozen with v0.5: the score version 2, which grows by extensions at the end of a list on each part instead of new versions (free time is the first); the editor's selection for plugins (`Event::Selection2`: the caret with its voice and string, beats of one voice, bars across tracks); tools that plugins add to the Tools menu (manifest `tools`); and changes plugins make to the score (`Host::score2` with its revision, `Host::apply`), each one step the user can undo, refused if the score changed in between. Plugins built for 1.0 and 1.1 keep working: they get version 1 and the old selection, and what version 1 can't hold is named among a file's losses. The SDK adds `FormatReader2`, `FormatWriter2` and `Tool`; the plugin guide has a new page, Tools and changes to the score. The Guitar Pro 7–8 plugin moves to 1.2.
- Bars in free time: read from and written to Guitar Pro 7–8 files, and never marked as bars that don't add up.
- Other programs open the .gp files OxiGuitar writes as it means them: on every pull request that touches the formats, alphaTab reads every fixture we write beat by beat as we do, and MuseScore 4 opens each and finds the same tracks, bars and notes (`.github/workflows/interop.yml`). Over the corpus, alphaTab reads all 104 files as we do, and MuseScore 4.7.5 the 97 it can open the originals of. For them, notes of tracks without strings now carry their pitch the way Guitar Pro 6 wrote it too, a bar with no beats is written with no voice, and a slide from a voice's last note, which has nothing to slide to and makes MuseScore crash, is left out as a named loss.
- `oxiguitar-cli convert FILE NEW-FILE`: reads a file and writes it in the format the new file's extension names (.gp), printing its losses. The test builds carry every reference song converted, the artifact "guitar-pro-check", for checking in Guitar Pro.
- The palette's voice buttons (1 to 4) work and show which voice the caret is in. With a single track on the page, Ctrl + Up and Ctrl + Down show the previous or next track of the song in its place, as Guitar Pro's single-track view does (Mateus's check of slice 1, 19 Oct 2026).
- `fixtures/guitar-pro-7.6/`: ten reference songs as Guitar Pro 7.6 saves them, and a test that reads each back against our original: every note, beat, effect and bar survives; Guitar Pro drops chord diagrams without a spelled-out chord and tempo text, and adds its own sounds and mixer settings (lessons for the .gp writer).

- The caret and the selection on the page (M3 slice 1): a click in the music or Guitar Pro 8's keys move a caret drawn over the page (the beat across its staves, and its string framed on the tablature); Shift selects, Ctrl + A selects all; the page scrolls to keep the caret in view; plugins that listen hear every move. The Edit menu (Undo, Redo, Select All) and the toolbar's Undo and Redo work: Tools → Infer Key Signatures is now a change you can undo. Bars that don't add up are laid in red on screen (not in a PDF), the footer says how many and where the first is, and Tools → Next Bar That Doesn't Add Up (F4) walks through them. The manual has a new page, Editing.
- The editor's foundations (M3 slice 1), without a window yet: `oxiguitar-edit`, with the caret and its moves as Guitar Pro 8's cursor makes them (beat by beat across bar lines, string by string, Home, Ctrl + Home and Ctrl + End, Tab to the next staff, another track keeping the same moment, another voice likewise), selections (a run of beats in one voice, or whole bars across tracks), edit commands with unlimited undo and redo that copy only the parts of the score they change, and the check for bars that don't add up: every bar when a file opens, then only the bars an edit touches. In the corpus, 231 of 83,721 staff bars don't add up, in 20 of 104 files, checked in about 60 ms. The engraver now also says where every staff, bar, beat and string is drawn, and lays a translucent red under the bars it is asked to mark (never in a PDF).

### Fixed

- Right-click on a track → Properties, Transpose or Delete crashed the app on Windows: the command now runs after the menu has closed (issue #102). Edit → Delete Track… is added.
- The Inspector counts a voice as Guitar Pro 8 does: "1.5/4", green when the bar is full and red when short or too long, and lists "notes and rests" entered instead of "beats" (issue #104).
- A selected rest's Dynamic drop-down filled the Inspector; it is a normal drop-down again (issue #105).
- Guitar Pro 3, 4 and 5 files: a track's volume and pan were read on the wrong scale (they run 0 to 16, not 0 to 127), brush strokes were read one speed too fast (a 16th was read as an 8th), and mute and solo of a Guitar Pro 5 track were not read (found by the .gp5 writer's round trips, 21 Oct 2026). (alphaTab reads the brush speeds one step shorter than Guitar Pro 5 writes them.)
- A file holding a bend at the very end of the possible range no longer stops OxiGuitar when it is saved as .gp (found by fuzzing, 21 Oct 2026).

- Pasting bars from a track without strings (a piano) into one with strings, or the other way, no longer keeps notes the track can't hold: saved as .gp, they came back on another string or as other notes (found by the editing gates, 21 Oct 2026).

- .gp files OxiGuitar writes show the staff at the right octave in Guitar Pro: Guitar Pro numbers octaves from MIDI 0 (MIDI 60 is C5), one above scientific pitch, and draws the staff from the written pitch, which was an octave low (Mateus's check in Guitar Pro 7.6, 20 Oct 2026).
- Tracks without strings (pianos, violins, voices) in .gp files OxiGuitar writes no longer show a tuning of six Cs and frets of −2147483648 in Guitar Pro: they get Guitar Pro's own default tunings and every note a string and fret, as Guitar Pro 7.6 writes them. Reading Guitar Pro 7–8 files, such instruments shown without tablature are read without strings; shown with tablature, they keep them.

### Changed

- Lyrics follow Guitar Pro's syntax on the page: each space or hyphen after the first leaves a note without a syllable (files that use several spaces for held notes now show them as Guitar Pro does), a plus joins two words on one note, and text in square brackets isn't drawn.

- Dynamics are hidden on the page by default (View → Dynamics; Mateus, 20 Oct 2026: they clutter the page).
- Ctrl + T no longer opens the Bar dialog: it types lyrics (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026). Ctrl + K, Ctrl + /, D, Shift + Insert and a double-click on a time signature still open it.
- PDFs, from the window and from `oxiguitar-cli render`, are drawn in pure black on white, with no grey or colour (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026).
- The .gp writer keeps a slide from a voice's last note to no note, as players slide off a note to nothing (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026); it used to leave it out as a loss, because MuseScore 4 crashes on it. The MuseScore check now leaves such files out and names MuseScore's bug.
- The .gp writer marks the note a hammer-on or pull-off goes to (`HopoDestination`), as Guitar Pro does: Guitar Pro 7.6 drew two arcs over each without it (Mateus's check, 20 Oct 2026).
- Each reference song says what it tests in its score information (Instructions), and `fixtures/guitar-pro-check/` keeps what the .gp writer makes of every reference song and of the test song in every format, for anyone to check in Guitar Pro or other programs (`CONTRIBUTING.md`, "Checking what OxiGuitar writes"; `scripts/guitar-pro-check.sh` writes them, a test keeps them current).
- Guitar Pro 6, 7 and 8 files: voices keep their places. An unused voice before a used one is read as an empty voice instead of the next one moving up into its place (voice 3 stays voice 3, stems down), and written as no voice; the .gp writer no longer names that as a loss. One bar of the corpus changes (Flor de Lis).
- Guitar Pro 7 and 8 files: an open string in a chord diagram that starts above the first fret is read as open, not at the diagram's first fret, and a diagram Guitar Pro is told to hide is read as a chord name only.
- A click on a track's name selects the track for the editor instead of showing it alone; a track is shown while its clef or TAB button is on (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026).
- The arrow keys, Home and End move the caret once a score is open; Page Up, Page Down and Space still scroll.
- The palette leaves out Guitar Pro 8's Automation group, its Timer and both its stem-direction tools: OxiGuitar draws stems by voice only, odd voices up and even voices down (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026). 124 tools remain, in five groups.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-19

Milestone M2 (the plugin system and the window shell) is complete. OxiGuitar is now a small core with plugins: every file format is a plugin, and the program alone opens nothing, so install the basic plugins beside it (the user manual's "Installing"). Plugin interface 1 is frozen from this release: plugins built for it keep loading in later versions. Writing your own plugin: the plugin developer guide (https://mateusrdp.github.io/oxiguitar/plugin-guide/), its template, and the SDK (`oxiguitar-plugin`, taken from this repository at the tag `v0.2.0`). The window shows where the editor and the player are going: the editor's palette and each track's mixer, greyed out until they arrive.

### Added

- A plugin that fails is withdrawn for the rest of the session, and OxiGuitar carries on as if it weren't installed (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026): a plugin that can't start, crashes, or breaks the interface's contract (an answer that can't be read or wasn't asked for, tab contents that can't be shown) loses its formats, tabs, services, instruments and events; a file it would have read goes to the next plugin that recognises it, and a service is chosen again among the others. The footer says so once, as an error, its tabs leave the window, and the plugin manager shows it as stopped working, and why. An error a plugin answers with is not a failure: a widget event it refuses is a warning in the footer, and the tab keeps its contents.
- The plugin developer guide (M2 slice 7), `docs/plugins/`, published beside the user manual at https://mateusrdp.github.io/oxiguitar/plugin-guide/: what a plugin is and isn't, the `.oxp` file and its manifest key by key, the SDK, formats (signatures, untrusted data, the score, import logs, losses), tabs (every widget, the canvas's display list, the pointer), events and the open score, services, instruments, settings, testing with `cargo test`, `oxiguitar-plugin-test` and `oxiguitar-cli`, building for every system, where users put the file, and the interface's versions. Its examples are the template's own code.
- The template plugin, `docs/plugins/template/`, to copy: a crate of its own that reads and writes a small text tab format (`.extab`), adds a Statistics tab in the right area (the notes on each string of a track, as a small chart to click; a toggle remembered in settings.json; score events), and a `note-names` service; it takes the SDK and the contract suite from this repository at the release's tag; its `cargo test` runs the contract suite on its `.oxp` file, and its GitHub workflow builds the file for all six targets and attaches them to a release. CI builds a plugin from it the guide's way on Linux, Windows and macOS, as part of `ci-success`.
- The window shows what's coming (M2 slice 6). The left panel holds the editor's palette, open from the start: every tool of Guitar Pro 8's editing palette, 132 in its six groups (voices and modes, bar, note, effects, notation, automation), each as an icon drawn by our renderer from Bravura's symbols, the interface font, lines and curves, centred on its ink, greyed out until the editor with a tooltip giving its name. Groups fold away with a click on their heading. Each track's row in the Tracks tab gains its mixer: mute and solo buttons drawn like the notation buttons, a volume slider and a pan knob, showing what the file sets, greyed out until an output plugin is installed.

- The test builds (Actions → Test builds) carry the test plugin apart, `oxiguitar-test-plugin-<system>`, with a README saying where to put it, for trying plugin tabs and events by hand.
- `debian/`: both packages, `oxiguitar` and `oxiguitar-plugins-basic`, built the way Debian builds them (dh-cargo, `dpkg-buildpackage`, `-dbgsym` packages), in sid with no network, by the Debian workflow, which also runs lintian on them and installs them into a clean sid. The source is `scripts/debian/source.sh`'s: HEAD without our Noto Sans, which comes from `fonts-noto-core` (`Built-Using`), plus the crates in a vendor component until Debian has them all (`docs/debian.md`).
- Manual pages for `oxiguitar` and `oxiguitar-cli` (`assets/linux/man/`), installed by the .deb and .rpm. Each .deb gains a Debian-format `changelog.gz` and `md5sums`, and the release workflow runs lintian from Debian sid on every .deb (`scripts/package/lintian.sh`): errors and warnings fail the release, but for the two tags about the contact being the issue tracker.
- The crates against Debian (`scripts/debian/crate-audit.py`, the Debian workflow): every crate the Linux programs and the basic plugins are built from, both builds and both processors, checked against the `librust-*-dev` packages of Debian unstable, weekly and on every change to `Cargo.lock`. The report is the run's summary; it fails nothing.
- The core and the basic plugins are packaged apart (M2 slice 5). No core package holds a plugin any more: the .deb and .rpm (both builds), the AppImages, the Windows zips and the .dmg are the program alone, and the core Suggests `oxiguitar-plugins-basic` (.deb and .rpm, into `/usr/lib/oxiguitar/plugins/`, for either Linux build). Every system also gets `OxiGuitar-plugins-basic-VERSION-SYSTEM-ARCH.zip`, the plugins with a README naming the plugin folders, and every plugin is attached to the release on its own for every target (`oxiguitar-gp5-VERSION-linux-x86_64.oxp` …). The release tests that each core package alone opens nothing and opens a Guitar Pro file once the plugins are added, and the draft release checks it has every package before it is made. Manual: "Installing" has the plugins.
- Two Linux builds (M2 slice 4): the Wayland build, the default and the main one, as the packages `oxiguitar` and `OxiGuitar-….AppImage`, and the X11 build, deprecated from the start, as `oxiguitar-x11` (which Provides, Conflicts with and Replaces `oxiguitar`) and `OxiGuitar-x11-….AppImage`. - Plugin tabs and pushed events (M2 slice 3; plugin interface 1.1, added at the end of every enum, so 1.0 plugins keep working). A plugin declares tabs in its manifest (an id, a title, one of the window's four areas) and fills them with standard widgets (labels, buttons, toggles, sliders, fields, drop-downs, lists) that the core draws in its own style, plus canvases it draws itself with a display list (lines, rectangles, paths, text placed with the core's fonts, colours by role) and receives pointer events on in its own units. Contents the core can't show are refused with a sentence saying why. A plugin subscribes to events (score opened, changed or closed; selection; playback position); the host pushes them in order on a thread of its own that sleeps until there is something to deliver, so nothing polls, and a slow plugin gets the latest position rather than a backlog. - Plugins load (M2, ADR 0008). The SDK for plugin authors, `oxiguitar-plugin` (MIT OR Apache-2.0): a plugin implements `Plugin` and the traits for what it contributes (`FormatReader`, `FormatWriter`, `Service`), writes `manifest.json`, and calls `export_plugin!`, which puts the manifest in the `oxg_manifest` section and exports the entry symbol; plugin authors write no `unsafe`. A panic in a plugin is caught and reported as an error, and the plugin goes on working. `cargo test` checks the manifest against the plugin (unknown keys, declared formats and services that aren't implemented, the version).
- The plugin host in `oxiguitar-host`: at start it reads every plugin's manifest with the `object` crate, which reads only the manifest's section (30 manifests in about 1 ms), and loads nothing. From the manifests it knows the formats that can be opened and saved and recognises a file by its signatures, offers instruments by the instrument classes they play, and finds services. A plugin is loaded the first time one of its contributions is used, and never unloaded. Services are chosen by their conditions (system, processor, display system), their priority and whether their probe says they work here, unless settings.json pins one (`services`); `disabled` in settings.json keeps plugins from loading; of several files holding the same plugin, the highest version wins (then the earlier folder). Plugins send footer messages, keep their own settings under their id in settings.json, and call services through the host. The footer names the plugins found and every plugin file that can't be used, and why (damaged, another system's, a newer interface).
- The plugin contract suite, `oxiguitar-plugin-test` (a library, a program for plugin authors, and the tests): builds a plugin as an `.oxp` file and checks it through the host (manifest read without loading, it starts, missing contributions are "not found", damaged input and an empty score never crash it, every service answers its probe). A test plugin (`plugins/test`, never shipped) runs every part of the interface end to end on every system CI tests, including errors, panics and a plugin calling a service through the host. A test checks that `unsafe` code is allowed only in the host's loader and the SDK's export glue, and CI's speed budget now includes reading 30 plugin manifests (under 10 ms).
- The plugin interface, `oxiguitar-plugin-api` (M2, ADR 0008), under MIT OR Apache-2.0 so plugins may use any GPL-compatible licence (Mateus, 18 Oct 2026): the two C tables a plugin and the host exchange, the messages that cross them (encoded with postcard, each enum's variant positions pinned by a test), the manifest (JSON in the `oxg_manifest` section, read without loading code; unknown keys ignored so newer plugins load in older hosts), format signatures that recognise a file from its bytes or its ZIP members, instrument classes for every General MIDI program, and the score as plugins see it (version 1, a mirror of the model with its encoding pinned). `oxiguitar-model`'s `wire` feature converts between the two: every fixture, visual reference and corpus file crosses without loss.

### Fixed

- Escape closes the Help notes (Keyboard and Mouse, About), as a click already did.
- View → Layout and File → Paper Size tick only the current choice.
- The score no longer blinks blank after a zoom, a new engraving (layout, tracks, paper, dynamics) or Dark Page: the old tiles, scaled to the new zoom, stay under the new ones until those cover the view.

### Changed

- The track list has no show tick any more: a track's notation buttons show and hide it (both off hides it, either button shows it again), which saves room and leaves one control per job.
- No View → Multitrack menu item either: showing a track's own buttons already shows it alongside the others, so the menu item did nothing the buttons didn't, for one more control to keep in sync.
- With Dark Page, the canvas behind the page is a touch darker, so it reads as its own grey rather than matching the menus and track list, while staying lighter than those.
- Guitar Pro 6–8 files open about twice as fast: the XML tree keeps every element in one list and borrows names and text from the file instead of copying them (`oxiguitar-cli info` on Seek and Destroy: 0.11 s instead of 0.26 s). The scores and import logs read from every GPX and GP file in the corpus are unchanged.
- Guitar Pro 6–8 beats keep their dynamic as a marking (`BeatEffects::dynamic`) as well as giving it to their notes as velocity, so the engraver can tell a marking from a velocity.
- Beat text at the end of a system no longer runs off the page; the page test now checks where each text ends, not only where it starts.
- Plan (Mateus, 16 Oct 2026): the core reads and writes Guitar Pro formats only. MuseScore, MusicXML, ABC and MIDI files, and tab derivation for staves without tablature, become plugins after the plugin system (M5). M1's "98 % of the corpus opens" counts Guitar Pro files only. Visual regression uses 50 generated reference scores kept in the repository. ROADMAP and README follow. Opening one of those files now says that a format plugin will open it, instead of "not supported yet".
- Voice 1's stems point up even when it is the only voice in the bar (Mateus, 16 Oct 2026). Stems on the staff now always follow the voice number.
- Stems follow the voice number, as Mateus asked after the first review: voices 1 and 3 up, voices 2 and 4 down, whenever a voice other than the first has notes in the bar (before, the first voice with notes went up and all others down, so voice 3 pointed down and a lone voice 2 pointed up). Drums always follow it; voice 1 alone still points its stems by where its notes sit. Rests of several voices move up or down by the same rule.
- Rhythm on tablature (tab-only tracks) shows every voice with the same rule: voices 1 and 3 with stems up above the tab, voices 2 and 4 with stems down below it. Before, only voice 1 was drawn, with stems down. Rests on tab move out of the staff into each voice's rhythm area, so they no longer cover fret numbers.
- Chord names stay over their beat. Instead of pushing a name sideways when it would touch the next one, the music opens up to make room. On tab-only tracks the chord names sit above the upward stems.
- Drum notes from Guitar Pro 3–5 files are kept as pitches (the General MIDI drum) rather than as string and fret, as the score model intends for instruments without strings.
