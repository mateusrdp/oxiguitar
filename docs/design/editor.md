# The editor (M3)

M3 makes OxiGuitar an editor: everything TuxGuitar edits, saved as .gp (the default) or .gp5. Its release, v0.5, is the first that Linux users can write tabs with, where until now they had TuxGuitar or Wine (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026). The plan and its slices are in [ROADMAP.md](../../ROADMAP.md) ("M3's slices, in order"); this page holds the parity checklist that decides when M3 is done, and the editing rules as they are decided.

## The TuxGuitar parity checklist

What TuxGuitar 1.6 lets a user edit, read from its menus and dialogs (helge17/tuxguitar, `desktop/TuxGuitar/src/app/tuxguitar/app/view/menu/impl/`, checked 19 Oct 2026), next to the brief's requirements (EDIT-1 to EDIT-10, TAB-1). An item is ticked when it can be entered from the palette or a menu and by its shortcut, changed, deleted, undone and redone, is drawn, and survives saving as .gp and .gp5 (or is listed as a .gp5 loss) and reading back. Mateus approved the list on 19 Oct 2026 ("looks good for now"); it can grow.

Where OxiGuitar goes past TuxGuitar (four voices to its two, any tuplet to its list), the brief's requirement is the item.

### Notes and rhythm (slice 3; tuplets in slice 4)

- [x] Notes entered on the standard staff: a pitch, its string chosen for the lowest playable fret near the caret's string (EDIT-2)
- [x] Chords: several notes in one beat
- [x] Rests; inserting a rest before the caret; deleting a note or a rest
- [x] Note values from whole to 64th; dotted and double-dotted
- [x] Tied notes

- [x] Four voices per staff (EDIT-3; TuxGuitar has two), moving a beat to another voice
- [x] Semitone up and down; the other spelling of a note (enharmonic); a note moved to the next string up or down at the same pitch
- [x] Moving beats left and right
- [x] Clearing a beat and a bar; removing a voice nobody uses

## Editing rules

Decided by Mateus on 19 Oct 2026 unless said otherwise.

- **Bars that don't add up** are allowed, shown and played (M4 adjusts what playback needs), only marked: on screen in red, never in a PDF. Every bar is checked when a file opens; after that only the bars an edit touches are checked again, nothing more ("I want this app to be FAST"). A voice holding only empty placeholder beats (Guitar Pro 3–5 fill an unused voice with one) doesn't count. The footer says how many bars and where the first is; Tools → Next Bar That Doesn't Add Up walks the caret through them.
- **Stems** go by voice only: odd voices up, even voices down. The palette has no stem-direction tools.
- **Transposition** has three ways in (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026), so no task needs a selection made first: right-click on a track's row → Transpose… (that track), right-click on a selection → Transpose… (the selection, on its track), Tools → Transpose… (every track).
- **Tracks:** a click on a row selects the track; a track is shown when its TAB or standard-notation button is on. Right-click on a row: Transpose…, Merge Into…, Delete (which asks first); the track's properties are the Track tab's (issue #177).
- **Tunings** are named compactly wherever they show (E Standard, Drop D, Open G, or the notes, low to high: `D A D G A D`). In the Track tab the tuning is a key and a pattern: Standard, Drop, Open with the root on the lowest string (Open D, Open E, Open C), Open with the fifth on the lowest string (Open G, Open A), or Other. Other keeps the key and takes any note for each string, checked only as a note and against the number of strings, never against the key.
- **The caret's voice** shows on the palette's voice buttons, highlighted (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026).
- **Ctrl + Up / Ctrl + Down** move among the shown tracks; with a single track shown, the previous or next track of the song takes its place, as in Guitar Pro's single-track view (Mateus, 19 Oct 2026).
- **The clipboard** is OxiGuitar's own: copying and pasting work inside OxiGuitar, not with other programs.
- **Undo** is unlimited (EDIT-5) and keeps copies of only what each command changed.
- **Note entry** (slice 3, `oxiguitar_edit::Command`) uses Guitar Pro 8's keys. A voice shorter than its bar gives the caret one more place after its last beat, where typing adds a beat as long as the one before it. A tied note always has the fret of the note it continues (the last note played on its string in the voice, as Guitar Pro reads a tie), so a change to a note carries through its ties. A typed note takes its beat's dynamic, or the last one before it (Guitar Pro keeps one per beat). A beat made a rest drops tapping, which belongs to notes. Voices keep their places: an unused voice before a used one is an empty placeholder, written as no voice. Every edit is held to two gates over every fixture (and the corpus where it is): random runs undone to the original and redone to the end, and the edited score saved as .gp reading back equal (`oxiguitar-edit --test entry`).
- **The standard staff** (slice 3): a click on it gives the caret a line or space, which Up / Down move and Enter fills, in the bar's key. Guitar Pro 8 offers its fretboard and keyboard views for this; OxiGuitar's caret is the MuseScore-like way in until those views come as plugins.
- **File → New** starts as Guitar Pro does: one bar, 4/4, 120, a steel-string guitar in standard tuning; Right at the end of the score adds a bar, as TuxGuitar does.
- **Bars** (slice 4, `oxiguitar_edit`'s `bars.rs`): a bar command works on the caret's bar or the selection's bars. Time and key signatures, feel and clefs change from the bar until the next bar that had another (Guitar Pro's "until the next change"); a repeat opens at the first bar and closes at the last; tempo is the song's tempo in the first bar, a change at the bar's start after it. Tempo changes inside a bar keep their fraction of the bar when its time signature changes (Guitar Pro keeps them so), on its 960-tick grid. Deleting bars never deletes the last one, moves the last tempo change in them to the bar after them, and re-ties tied notes after them to the note they now continue. A clef keeps its staff's octave (Guitar Pro keeps the octave per track); drum tracks keep the drums' clef. A change in the Bar tab is one change, recorded as the master bars and the caret's staff from the bar on. Keys as Guitar Pro 8's: Insert, Ctrl + Delete, `[`, `]`, `|` / Alt + Shift + L, `/`; Ctrl + K, Ctrl + /, D and Shift + Insert show the Bar tab, which holds what Guitar Pro spreads over five dialogs. Guitar Pro's Ctrl + T (time signature) types lyrics in OxiGuitar, and the time signature has no key of its own (Mateus, 20 and 21 Oct 2026).
- **The clipboard** (slice 4, `oxiguitar_edit`'s `clip.rs`), as Guitar Pro 8's: a run of beats copies as beats (the caret's beat with no selection) and pastes into any voice of any track; a selection across tracks copies whole bars with their master bars and pastes as bars, from the caret's track down, other tracks getting empty bars. Paste inserts at the caret, or at the selection's start; Special Paste sets how many times, and whether to replace instead. Replacing beats removes as much music as they last, and the part of the last removed beat they don't cover stays as rests; replacing bars overwrites from the caret's bar, adding bars at the end. Between drum and pitched tracks only rests carry over (Guitar Pro: "only the rhythm represented by rests is retained"); notes on strings the track lacks are dropped, and so are notes placed by pitch pasted into a track with strings or by string into one without (slice 6: a .gp file would read them back the other way); chord diagrams travel with their beats (found or added in the target track). Cut is a delete named "Cut". **Since #267 and #287 (Mateus, 15 Nov 2026):** Special Paste is gone (no more "times" or "replace"); Paste of beats always replaces from the caret and flows over bar lines, each bar filled to its time signature and a beat a bar line cuts split into tied pieces (`fit::pieces`), bars added at the end as needed, so it never overflows a bar; pasted bars (Ctrl + C across tracks) still go in before the caret's bar, and tracks the clip doesn't reach get bars of rests. Copy, Cut and Paste Bars (Ctrl + Shift + C, X, V, each with a small dialog) take every bar the selection touches, of the caret's track or of all tracks (`copy_bars`); Cut Bars deletes them from every track (bars are shared); Paste Bars inserts them before the first or after the last selected bar, each copied track into the track it came from (`ClipTrack::from`) with all tracks, or into the caret's track only (its own copy, else the first), other tracks getting bars of rests.
- **Tracks** (slice 5, `oxiguitar_edit`'s `tracks.rs`): Add Track puts a steel-string guitar after the caret's track and moves the caret to it; Duplicate names the copy "X (copy)"; Delete never deletes the last track. The track dialog is one change. Changing the strings or tuning keeps each note's pitch on its own string when it fits, else on a free string of its beat at the fret nearest where it was; or keeps the frets, letting the pitches change. Notes no string can play are left out after a question naming how many. Identical chord diagrams left after restringing are merged. The window follows each track where it went (`was` in the worker): shown tracks stay shown after tracks are added, deleted or moved, a new track is shown with the caret on it, and a caret left on a hidden track goes to the nearest shown one. An edit that changes the track list waits for the window's new list of shown tracks before engraving.
- **Transposition** (slice 5, `transpose.rs`): by semitones, or by steps of the key (the major scale of the key's tonic, so the music stays in its key). Keeping the fingering keeps each note on its string; otherwise each note goes to the fret nearest the one it had, so a run stays in position. Notes past a string's range or MIDI's are left out after a question. Every track (Tools → Transpose…) moves the key signatures too, choosing six flats over six sharps; a track or a selection leaves them. Drum tracks are never transposed. A right-click on the page moves the caret there unless it falls inside the selection; Transpose… there acts on the selection, or the caret's beat.
- **Merging tracks** (#312, Mateus, 18 and 19 Nov 2026; `oxiguitar_edit`'s `merge.rs`, the app's `MergeDialog`): right-click on a track's row → Merge Into… (also Edit → Merge Track Into…) writes that track's notes (the donor's) into another track (the receiver), then deletes the donor: one change, recorded whole. The dialog offers every other track of the donor's kind (a drum track goes only into a drum track, a pitched one only into a pitched one), the donor's voices to take as tickboxes (those with notes ticked), the receiver's voice they go into, and "Discard unisons". **Voices** (Mateus): one voice ticked goes into the voice picked; several go voice by voice (voice N into voice N), the voice list then locked. The donor's staff N goes into the receiver's staff N, or its last. **Rhythms** (Mateus: split and tie): beats are cut at every moment either track strikes or ends a note, and the pieces of a cut note are tied, written as Fit Bars writes them (`fit::spell`, `fit::piece`), so both sound as before; the merged voice lasts as long as the longest voice merged, rests included, and a voice of placeholders only counts as no voice. A bar whose pieces no note value can show (in the corpus, 1 bar: plain 64ths against triplet 16ths) stops the merge with a warning naming it. **Ranges** (Mateus, 19 Nov 2026): a track merges only into one whose range holds every note merged (the voices ticked, each staff against the receiver's staff it goes to; lowest open string to highest fret, with the capo; a staff without strings takes any pitch), so a bass goes into a guitar only once transposed. Otherwise nothing is merged: a question "Incompatible tracks" with OK alone, and the footer says how many notes of which track the other can't play, the other's range, and to consider transposing first (`MergeError::Ranges`). **Strings** ("keep the originals"; Mateus: fingering is for later): a donor note keeps its string and fret where the receiver's tuning plays its pitch there and the string is free; else it goes, at its pitch, to the free string nearest its fret (as the Track tab's restringing does, `tracks::replace`); else (every string that plays it taken at that moment) it is left out, after a question naming how many (a tied run counts once). Tied notes are placed first and stay on the string of the note they continue, or are struck again where it isn't free; a tie whose string another note took in between (some files tie over beats) is struck again, so no tie ever continues another pitch. Grace notes take strings nothing is sounding on. On a drum track notes stay drum keys, never strings (Guitar Pro 3 to 5 give drum staves six strings tuned to nothing). **Unisons** (Mateus): with "Discard unisons", a donor note is left out when the receiver strikes the same pitch at the same moment (a held receiver note doesn't count), with its ties. **What else goes:** where both play, a beat keeps the receiver's own marks (text, chord, stroke, dynamic); where only the donor plays, the donor's, its chord diagrams joining the receiver's. The donor's name, mixer and lyrics go with it; the receiver's lyrics get an empty beat for each new note their voice sings, so each syllable stays on its note (`lyrics::with_empty_beat`). Other tracks that showed the donor's chords show the receiver's (`merge::remove_track`, which Delete Track uses too, so track numbers kept elsewhere, chords shown from another track and per-track bar colours, follow both). Checked, before the range rule, by merging every pair of tracks of each corpus file three ways (4,788 merges): no panic, struck notes all at their moment and pitch or counted lost, bars that added up still do, no tie on another pitch.
- **Voices** (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026): only the caret's voice is drawn in full ink (black, or white on a dark page); the other voices are greyed. A PDF draws every voice, and everything in it, in pure black (`Theme::PRINT`).
- **Drum tracks** take no frets: their notes are drum sounds, entered with the drum tools later.
- **Effects** (slice 6, `oxiguitar_edit`'s `effects.rs`; Mateus, 20 Oct 2026): an effect is switched on or off. A note effect acts on the note on the caret's string and does nothing when that string has no note in the beat; on a staff without strings, on every note of the beat. A beat effect acts on the caret's beat. With a selection, every note (or beat) selected gets it, unless all of them have it already: then it comes off all of them. Rests take no effects, but dynamics. Effects that need strings (slides, bends, harmonics, vibrato, palm mute, let ring, trills, hammer-ons, tapping, slapping and popping, strokes, the whammy bar) apply only to notes on strings, never on drum tracks. A note slides in one way and out one way, and has one harmonic: another of the same side or kind replaces it. An effect with a value starts at a default, which the Inspector changes: a trill with the next fret up in sixteenths, tremolo picking in thirty-seconds (as Guitar Pro 8's key), a brush over a sixteenth, an artificial or tapped harmonic 12 up. A grace note becomes a thirty-second, as Guitar Pro writes them (83 of the corpus's 83 grace notes in .gp files are 32nds or 64ths). Tremolo picking is kept on the beat, as .gp files hold it. A dynamic is set, never switched off: it goes on the beat (notes and rests) and its notes play at it; there is always one in force.
- **Effect keys** (Mateus, 20 Oct 2026): Guitar Pro 8's keys, and for the effects it gives none, the effect's first letter with at most one modifier, the commonest guitar effects first; without a free one, no key. T is tapping (Guitar Pro's `)` too), Shift + T Text and Ctrl + T lyrics (21 Oct 2026); Alt + S shift slide and Shift + S slide out downwards (S stays Guitar Pro's legato slide; the corpus's .gp files have 178 shift slides, 77 legato, 21 out downwards, 15 in from below, 1 in from above); Alt + P pinch harmonic (P and Shift + P stay palm mute); Alt + T tapped harmonic. No key: slides in from below and above and out upwards, the semi-harmonic, popping, and the dynamics.
- **The bend editor** (slice 6, `bend_form.rs` in the app): as Guitar Pro 8's Bend window, a type, a pitch from ¼ tone to 3 tones, and a curve dragged on a grid of twelfths of the note by quarter tones. The curve is always an origin, a middle held at one pitch between two places, and a destination: the shape .gp files hold, so what the editor shows is what is saved. A curve read from a file that has another shape opens as its first, furthest and last points, as the .gp writer keeps it. The whammy bar's editor is the same, down to −3 tones and up to +3.
- **The Inspector's note and beat** (slice 6, `effect_form.rs` in the app; Mateus, 20 Oct 2026: the Inspector edits them, and effects with a value start at a default it changes): under the caret's bar, the note on the caret's string and its beat, as rows of tickboxes, drop-downs, numbers and buttons. A tickbox sends the effect's own command, so it acts as its key does (on the selection too); a drop-down or a number sets a value (`oxiguitar_edit::NoteChange`, `BeatChange`): accent, slide in and out, harmonic and its interval, trill speed and fret, fingering, dynamic, tremolo speed, grace note, brush direction and speed, pick stroke, technique. Bend and Whammy bar open the editor. Trills faster than sixteenths are allowed; a .gp file names them as a loss (GPIF has no trill speed), and .gp5 holds them.
- **Chords** (slice 6; `oxiguitar_edit::harmony`, the app's `chord_form.rs`; Mateus, 20 Oct 2026: with Guitar Pro 8's builder): a chord is a root (17 spellings, C to B with sharps and flats), a type (major, minor, augmented, diminished, sus2, sus4, power chord), a sixth, seventh or extension (6, 6/9, 7, maj7, 9, maj9, 11, 13, add9; 7 on a diminished triad is dim7), alterations (♭5, ♯5, ♭9, ♯9, ♯11, ♭13) and a bass; its name is written in ASCII as tabs write it (`C7(b9)/E`, `Am(maj7)`, `Bb(add9)`), which the .gp writer spells for Guitar Pro. A name the builder doesn't make is kept as typed. (Since #278 the chord dialog no longer shows the builder; Harmonize still builds its chords with it. See the next item.) The ways to play it: every shape within four frets that sounds every note (the fifth, and the ninth and eleventh of an eleventh or thirteenth, may be left out), its bass lowest, at least three strings (two for a power chord), at most four fingers with a barre that no open string lies under; ranked by fingers, strings skipped between played ones, stretch, height and strings not played. The track keeps each diagram once: one it has is used again (as the .gp reader keeps them). `Command::Chord` puts a diagram on the caret's beat, or takes it off (the diagram stays in the track's list); on a rest, or the place after the voice's last beat, a diagram with frets puts its notes in the beat, as Guitar Pro 8 does on an empty beat. A diagram's frets are absolute (0 open, `None` not played), never below its first fret.
- **Chord names and styles** (#278; `oxiguitar_edit::chord_names`, the app's `chord_form.rs` and `chord_prefs.rs`). Mateus asked for an existing Rust library for names to notes and back, chosen in this order: already a dependency, no conflict with ours and our toolchain, Rust, fast, well rated, mature, maintained. None was a dependency. **chordparser 4.0.4** (Pau Sala, MIT; Mateus, 15 Nov 2026) over tonal_rs 0.1.0 (a three-month-old port of tonal.js, regex and strings) and rust-music-theory (no free-form names, no notes to names): a hand-written lexer and parser that reads most conventions (`Em7-5`, `Eø`, `C△7`, `CM7`, `F-`, `G°7`, `D+`), gives each chord one normalised spelling (`Emi7(b5)`), and names a set of notes on each of them as the root (`inference`). It adds two small crates (enum-bitset and its derive). 900,000 random names and note sets raised no panic, in debug and release. What we do around it: a chord's *kind* is the normalised spelling without root and bass, and two names are the same chord when root, kind and bass agree; roots of names we make are spelled as Harmonize spells them (the library's normalised name loses a bass note's accidental, so we never use its bass); one note alone is named by its note. The ways to play a name are `harmony::shapes`, the builder's search, on the library's notes. **The dialog's rule** (#278): diagram and voicing are the same frets (inline notation, low string first, `x` not played, a space between strings so two-digit frets read clearly; typed without spaces every fret is one digit, and strings not reached are not played); when the frets change the name stays if it reads as one of their names, else becomes the first; when the name changes the frets stay if they play it (`Reading::is_played_by`), else become the first way to play it; a name the library can't read changes nothing else. **Styles** (#264): `chord-styles` in settings.json, a name and aliases from a kind to a text; never built in (Mateus: so they can be changed without building again), `chord-styles.json` is only what a settings.json without the key is given (Compact, Custom, Jazz, Popular, after Open Music Theory's and the OnMusic Dictionary's tables of chord symbols). Keys are compared by meaning and whole (Mateus, 15 Nov 2026): a key is read by the library like any name, so `m7b5`, `m7(b5)` and `ø` are one key, and a name whose kind has no alias stays as written. A name is read first as the picked style writes it (so a style's own text wins over the library's reading of it), then by the library, then as the other styles write it (for texts of the user's own the library can't read), which is what lets a song go from any style to any other. Picking a style rewrites the dialog's names; **Apply** sends `Command::RenameChords`, every track's diagrams renamed as one step. The model has no "diagram hidden" flag, so "Add diagram" off stores the name alone, as before. Harmonize's kept chords (`Chord::parse`) read names in other conventions through the same library. The Guitar Pro and MusicXML writers spell a chord from its name with small readers of their own, which know `ø`, `°`, `+` and `-` but not `△`: a possible later change there.
- **Text** (`Command::Text`): the text above the caret's beat (a note or a rest), trimmed; empty for none. Shift + T (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026; T is tapping).
- **Lyrics are typed on the page, as in MuseScore** (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026; `Command::Syllable`, `oxiguitar_model::lyrics`, the app's `Lyric` in `editing.rs`): Ctrl + T (Guitar Pro's time signature key) opens a field under the caret's beat on the page, on line 1 at the first beat the track's first voice sings from the caret's moment; Space and hyphen set the syllable and go to the next sung beat (a hyphen means the word goes on), Backspace in an empty field goes back, Up and Down change line (up to five, a new one under the last once the current holds something), Enter, Escape or a click elsewhere end. No lyrics dialog (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026). The model keeps Guitar Pro's lines: a text from a bar, a syllable per sung beat (first staff, first voice, with notes, not a grace note, not all tied) in order. Guitar Pro's syntax is followed exactly (its guide, "Lyrics Editing"): a space or a hyphen ends a syllable, each further one leaves a beat empty, as does one before the first syllable; a run of line breaks is one space; a plus joins words on one beat; square brackets hold a comment, and space right after one is no empty beat (Mateus, 21 Oct 2026: follow Guitar Pro, so files with several spaces show gaps, as in Guitar Pro). Typing a syllable changes only its own place in the text, keeping comments and line breaks; a beat past the end is reached with spaces, at the start with hyphens (a reader trims spaces at the ends). A new line starts at the beat's bar; typing before a line's start moves its start there. A line left with no syllable goes (a file can't keep an empty line in its place), and those after it move up. The caret follows the field, and every edit leaves it where typing goes on.
